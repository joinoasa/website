OASA doesn't rent or run spaces. Members start chapters inside existing makerspaces.

## Requirements

- A Lead-tier member to serve as chapter lead
- A host makerspace that signs a partnership agreement
- At least three interested members
- Approval: the founder during the founding phase, later the Chapters council

An approved chapter gets a name, a page, a channel and a chapter kit.

## Roles

Chapter lead, safety officer, Academy coordinator and outreach (optional). Small chapters can combine roles.

## Rhythm

- Monthly build night
- Workshops
- Demo days a few times a year
- Field test days at approved sites

## Chapter kit

The kit is being prepared. It is designed to include how to start a chapter, starter projects and reference builds, Academy curriculum, a recommended equipment list (RF bench, test cage, test bench), safety guidelines, branding and event templates, and a chapter page on joinoasa.org.

## Makerspace agreement

It covers what OASA brings, what the makerspace provides, safety and insurance, and how to end it. Members pay the makerspace's normal dues.

## Safety

- Store and charge lithium batteries by the makerspace's rules.
- Test flights and vehicles only at approved sites, following FAA and local rules.
- Supervise any young visitors around tools and testing (membership is 18+ for now).
- Report incidents.

## Reporting

Each chapter sends a short quarterly update.

## Together

Projects are global, and chapters share results, kits and lessons. An annual OASA Summit is planned, starting in Northern Virginia, with demos, grand challenges and talks.

## No makerspace nearby?

Start a satellite group anywhere members can meet, such as a garage, school or library, then grow into a chapter. The same safety rules apply.

## If a chapter goes quiet

Inactive chapters close. Members stay members, and a chapter can restart.
